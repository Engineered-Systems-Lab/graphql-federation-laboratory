## The transition

Study 2 needs its stacks to run against the real, Oracle-backed Missions service instead of the simulator, and the preflight kit (`study/preflight/`) states the thresholds a go rests on. Before this Job none of its steps had run on this machine. Every operator action and every `--run` waited for the lead developer's go after the step's printed plan.

- **Steps 1 to 3.** The inventory passed (the service image `starfleet-missions-service:1.0.0` was built with `docker build`, fully cached, because `docker compose build` needs the credentials). Oracle was restarted with `docker stop --time 600` and `docker start`, since the operator tool has no restart, and was ready in 13.3 s. Release 1.2 was reset (300 missions, 973 changes) and the data step passed; its clock check after the first block passed too.
- **Step 4.** The real and the simulated services each served 300 missions, and the six list pages are equal as parsed XML. `compare` found 200 differences (below).
- **Step 5.** The simulated alpha soak failed host-free-min: the minimum sampled host free memory was 3.38 GiB, while the maximum sampled container-memory total was 3.14 GiB. The operator's explanation is memory taken by host applications; the kit measures no breakdown of the rest. The lead developer chose to keep that result as measured and to close heavy applications before the real soaks. The router and adapter gates then ran once each and passed, outside the kit: the Apollo (35 tests) and Cosmo (36) router stack gates, the four adapter offline gates and the Missions, Assets and Personnel service gates; `gates-index.json` gives each one's command, main commit, exit code, result line and the SHA-256 of its log, which stays outside the repository. The real soaks of all five scenarios passed every threshold: a minimum sampled host free memory of at least 7.04 GiB, a maximum sampled container-memory total of at most 3.12 GiB, no OOM kill or unplanned restart and no request error. Under the kit's common probes, the soak medians were about 0.44 to 0.47 s for the router's full page and 17 to 26 ms for the Missions REST pages; these are not agent-level latencies of any arm.
- **Step 6.** Every outage passed on all five scenarios, with no router restart. Each backend was seen down 1.02 to 1.06 s after its stop. It answered again 0.02 to 0.47 s after its start command returned, and 4.03 to 10.43 s after that command began (the longest for Personnel).
- **Step 8.** `make_config.py --missions real` wrote the study config outside the repository, and provenance on epsilon passed: the observed stack matches the declaration and Missions is real as intended and as observed.

## Decision L and the go

The worker grouped the 200 differences and proposed a classification against the template's five questions; the lead developer accepted it, and at their review asked that the record separate what differs from what needs changing. The groups are serialization (48), illustrative error message text with equal error codes (2), HEAD and unknown-path answers (118) and the features the simulator lacks (32: `/missions/{id}/changes`, the ETag, the status filter, Accept and trailing-slash checks); latency is recorded beside them. In every group the responses differ. The differences include tool-visible response changes (status, content type, body) and transport headers that the raw tools do not return (Server, Content-Length, Transfer-Encoding, Allow, ETag); the report marks each one. No corrective setup change was identified for scored runs, which use the real service: no change to the tool configuration, the guide, the reference solutions or the answer key. Where the two backends disagree, the real service's answers agree with study 1's frozen guide (`study/guide/enterprise-legacy-systems-guide.md`) where that guide specifies the behaviour. Guide accuracy and answerability stay pending their own checks: the guide's check under decision J, and step 7. The simulator is recorded as unfit for the group D paths. Every difference has a known cause.

The lead developer's judgement, in the wording they chose: "The backend preflight holds for the real Missions backend: every real soak and outage passed on all five scenarios; the one host-free-min failure is the simulated alpha soak under host-application memory pressure, not the stack; decision L found no unexplained discrepancy. Answerability (step 7) and the paid smoke test (step 9) remain for later Jobs."

Their go or no-go: "Go for the real Missions backend, on the condition that steps 7 and 9 pass in their own Jobs and that heavy host applications are closed during scored runs."
