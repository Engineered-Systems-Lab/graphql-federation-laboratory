- **The corpus manifest**, written with `derive_a.py --write --frozen 2026-11-06`: status `frozen-2026-11-06`, SHA-256 `c57081e3fbcaaf298fc3591b486930fa4fb5dd88a0772f3c29798e26a1a354bb`. It lists `tasks.json`, `schemas.json` and `expected.json`, so it pins every file the scored run reads. What the manifest pins is not yet what the runner enforces. Checking those files against the frozen manifest before the paid run starts or resumes is code, reported for before the paid run. The four derived files keep their draft stamp, and the manifest's status governs. No task, key or wording changed.

## The power, and why study 1's figures stand

The development runs hold only 8 tasks, with the tool versions mixed. Their τ came out smaller than study 1's for all three contrasts: 5.2, 7.3 and 0.0 points against 16.6, 23.1 and 5.9. The frozen figures are therefore study 1's under the plan's rules at 48 tasks: P1 65.0 and P2 51.7 percent at a true difference of 0, and 32.5 and 22.0 percent at 5 points.

The development table is reported beside them. It is not a gentler picture everywhere. At 5 points it gives P1 less power (24.7 percent), and its 8 tasks, drawn with replacement to 48, leave many simulated fits unusable (49 of 400 at 0 points).

## The limits

The lead developer kept the limits (decision Z5). In the seven paid development runs, 17 runs stopped at the token limit, all but one on V05, and one stopped at the tool-call limit. None reached 900 s. In version 5's confirmation one run in 32 crossed 1 M tokens.

The coverage line that says several pages add up to the total while no page states its place stays in the tool. At the lead developer's request it is noted among version 5's known limits, as cautious rather than redundant: it appears on correct calculations too.
